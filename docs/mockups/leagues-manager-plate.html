Five ways off the glass

The plate at the top of the leagues page is the last translucent surface on that screen. Everything under it went machined — the subject rail is a .lab-slab, the heading billet is a .lab-ledge, and the league cards left LIST_ROW_SURFACE for the trades board's own corner-lit block. The card didn't come with them.

Every option below carries exactly the content the card carries today — season tab, stat tab, avatar, name, record digits, scope, denominator, proportion bar, win-pct dial, sync line. Only the surface changes.

What is actually wrong with it

the diagnosis
the tell

The card is glass on the outside and machined on the inside. Its two corner tabs are already .lab-well, and the kickoff countdown's digit cells are already .lab-readout.

So it is not that the app has two materials — it is that this one part wears both, four surfaces deep, with the join running around its own edge.

the neighbours

Directly below it sit three machined parts in a row: the subject rail (slab, chamfered, wall down and right), the heading billet, then a hundred league cards.

Reading down the page, the plate is the only thing you could put a finger through.

the aspect trap

.lab-slab-face's corner-lit fill runs at 168°. Over this card's box (~1200×130) the gradient line is only ~380px long, so the three stops resolve inside the leading third and the rest sits flat on #0a1723 — three values off the page ground.

That is the exact failure .lab-slab-face-rail was written for. It is why B and C differ, and why the difference is worth seeing rather than reasoning about.

What it buys

The material the app already uses for a surface holding an instrument: it is what an expanded league card becomes, and what the detail panel's body is made of. So the header is now the same face the list turns into when you open a row.

Nothing about the layout, the corner tabs, the rail, the sweep or the sync line changes — which means nothing about the card's height, its wrapping or its phone behaviour changes either.

diff · one class, one deleted gradient

What it costs

It is the quietest of the five. Rounded corners and a bottom-only wall put it in a different family from the chamfered, two-sided-wall parts immediately below it — coherent, but not obviously one instrument with them.

If the complaint is "this doesn't fit", A fixes it. If the complaint is "the top of the page doesn't read as one thing", A does not.

What it buys

Maximum continuity with the list. Same wall, same chamfer, same brushed grain — the header and the cards are visibly the same kind of object, and the corner tabs pick up the chamfer for free because the slab's clip-path clips its whole subtree.

What it costs

Look at the trailing half of the face. The 168° corner-lit fill resolves inside the leading third of a box this wide and this short, so the right-hand end sits flat on a tone barely above the page ground — the stat tab and the dial appear to float off the part. That is the documented reason .lab-slab-face-rail exists, and it lands on this card too.

Two smaller ones. It needs .lab-slab-fixed, or the header lifts and blooms under the cursor promising a press that does nothing. And a slab is an object in a list — this one is not in the list, which is a claim the material makes and the card can't keep.

risk · the fill degenerates at this aspect ratio

What it buys

The three parts at the top of the page stop being three unrelated objects. Header, rail and billet share a wall, a chamfer family and a light direction, and they read down the page as one console with the list running out from under it.

It is also the only slab option whose fill is correct at this box's aspect ratio — the horizontal graded base and the scaled falloffs keep the trailing end clear of the page ground, which is exactly what the stat tab and the dial sit on.

fixes B's trailing edge for free

What it costs

The face is a step lighter than a league card's, so the header is the brightest thing on the page. That is arguably right — it is the page's subject — but it is a deliberate choice, not a neutral one.

It also needs .lab-slab-fixed, and the four-corner chamfer means the corner tabs have to be re-cut to follow it rather than keeping their 15px outer radius.

What it buys

It commits to what the card already says about itself. Its four corners are readouts; this makes its middle one too, so the whole part obeys one rule — recessed means read me — instead of stating it at the corners and dropping it in the body.

It is also the only option where the name gets a housing rather than floating, which is what stops a long username reading as loose text on a wide face.

What it costs

Depth budget. The house rule is three countable z-levels on a card. This spends all three — plate, cut, dial rim — so there is nothing left, and anything added later has to displace something.

And it leaves dead face. Once the housing hugs its contents — which it has to, or it is 500px of empty trough — there is a wide blank stretch between it and the dial. On a chassis that is arguably correct (an instrument panel has unused face), but it is real estate the glass card was not spending, and it grows with the viewport.

It is the largest diff of the five: new housings, new padding, and the record's cyan scope word has to be re-checked against a darker floor. The em-dash and no-records states need re-checking too, since an empty housing reads differently from empty text.

What it buys

It answers the complaint by subtraction: there is no surface left to disagree with anything. The page opens on its subject, then on its first real part — which is the same argument that took the page heading off /trades.

It gives the list back roughly 24px of chrome, and it is the only option with no material to maintain.

gives the list ~24px back

What it costs

The corner tabs lose the edge they were machined into, so they become floating chips — the one thing this app's grammar says a fact must not be. They would have to become something else, which is content work rather than a restyle.

The sync line also loses the box that scoped it, and the record's proportion bar loses the field it was measured against. Both are legible here and both got quieter.

Where this lands

the recommendation

C, with A as the safe version of the same idea

C is the one that answers the actual complaint. The card doesn't fit because everything around it is a machined part with a wall and a chamfer and it is a pane of glass; C makes it the same kind of part as the rail directly beneath it, and the three of them read down the page as one console. It is also the only slab option whose fill is right at this box's proportions — which is the thing B gets wrong, visibly, at its trailing edge where the stat tab and the dial live.

A is the low-risk version. One class swapped, no geometry touched, no chamfer to re-cut, and it lands on a material the app already uses for exactly this job — an expanded league card is this face. If the goal is "stop it looking like glass" and nothing more, A is finished in an afternoon and cannot regress anything.

D and E are real but they are different questions. D is a redesign of what the card is, not of what it is made of, and it spends the whole depth budget doing it. E is worth taking seriously if the honest answer is that this card is chrome the page could do without — but it turns the two corner tabs into a content problem, so it isn't a restyle either.
