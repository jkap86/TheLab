Trades board · the player / manager search · four seatings

The search is two presses deep and asks you to know the answer first

Looking a player up on the trades board takes a press on Filters, a scroll past the scope keys, the window and the match mode, and then a choice between three separate search boxes — you have to know whether the name you are typing is a player, a manager or a pick before you can type it. That is the one thing on this page every reader arrives wanting, sitting behind the control built for the things they don't.

Four ways to put it on the page, drawn in the app's own faces over the real board. Each is shown at laptop width and at 390px, because the ledge's row is already carrying five things and the crowding only shows up at the width it happens at. Every height quoted below is measured off these renders, not estimated — which is how the cheapest-looking option turned out to be the expensive one on a phone.

What the search costs today

Read first

The ledge is right about most of what it holds. The window, the circle and the match mode are settings — chosen once, read afterwards — and a control that expands in place is the honest seat for them. The three facet lists are not settings. They are the question, and they are filed with the settings because that is where the dialog they came from put them.

What it costs

The field and three controls fight for one row. Below ~700px it takes a line of its own anyway — measured at 390px this is 171px, the same as B, which is B with the two storeys in the other order and B's version is the tidier one.

True in all four

Decisions that don't depend on the seat

One field over all three kinds

Players, managers and picks in one box, results grouped under captions. A reader types a name, not a category — and the group heading still says which it found. This is what the manager tool's rail already does across players and leaguemates.

Picks come out too, or they become the lost one

If two of the three lists move onto the page and picks stays in the modal, picks is the filter nobody finds again. 2027 1st matching by round and season is one line of matching over the same facet list.

The panel floats; it never pushes the board

The ledge sits inside the element the virtualizer measures, so anything that expands in place moves every card. Floating over the list is what the manager rail's panel and both ADP floats already do.

It commits live — no draft, no Apply

Each result carries how many trades name it, counted without the current selection, so pressing one can't move the number beside it. That is what makes committing on press safe here and not in the league filters.

Facets are still fetched on open

The season-wide grouped aggregate behind those counts costs ~850ms. Mount the panel on press, exactly as the ledge does today, so arriving at the page still costs one page of cards and nothing else.

The all/any key travels with the selection

It belongs to the subjects, not to the window or the circle — so it goes where the tokens go: the panel's footer (A, C, D) or the storey's trailing end (B). What stays in Filters is the window and the circle.

If you want a recommendation

C. This page's default is every crawled league and 50,000 trades — nobody reads that; they arrive with a name. A control that has to be discovered is the wrong shape for the gesture that is the page's whole purpose, and C is the only one of the four a reader cannot fail to see.

Building it changed the argument against it. The case for A was that it is free, and it is — on a laptop. At 390px A is 120px at rest and 153px once two subjects are picked, because tokens land among the controls and shove them onto new lines; C is 133px and 136px, because they wrap inside the field and nothing else moves. So the 41px C costs on a laptop is bought back on the width that has the least room, and the option that looked like the cheap one is the one that grows.

D is the interesting second, and only in its free spelling — a real field on the existing row with the scope sentence deleted rather than rehoused. Keep the sentence and it costs 39px for a field half of C's width. B is the most disciplined shape and the tallest thing here (117px, 171px on a phone); it is the answer if the selection should be able to run to six or seven tokens. A is the cheapest to build and the easiest to miss.
