Four pills that never say they nest, over a date that scrolls away

The ordering

Widest, then narrowest, then two middles. No reading of the row tells you which circle is bigger than which — and the two leaguemate keys differ by one word for a distinction the caption has to carry.

The dead keys

Three of the four are inert without a stored account, each drawn full size, each pointing at the same sentence. The keys stay visible on purpose — a filter nobody can find is one nobody knows they could have — but three of them is two too many ways to say it.

The cost of scrolling

The seek is a place, not a setting: it is worth reaching for while reading, which is exactly when the block is three screens up. Today, travelling means scrolling back to a control you last touched an hour ago.

What it buys

It is the only option that teaches the nesting, and it teaches it with a length rather than a sentence: pick Mine and a quarter of the track is lit, pick All and it runs to both ends. The one thing the four pills could never say.

It is the app's existing slider grammar — a recessed track with a raised key riding in it, the ADP drawer's value curve — at four fixed detents. Nothing new to learn, and one press still reaches any circle.

Inert without an account reads as an unlit run rather than three dead pills: the thumb parks on All and the first three detents simply never light.

It is not the height play, and the measurements say so — against today's at 390px, . What it buys is that the keys stop wrapping and the row starts meaning something; B and C are where the height is.

What it costs

The labels have to fit one line, so Leaguemate trades becomes Leaguemates and Leaguemate leagues becomes Their leagues. The note under the rail therefore has to speak always, where today it goes quiet on the widest circle.

A flood can be misread as "all four are on". What stops it is that the selection is a lit key and the run behind it a dim wash — two materials, not one tone. Flatten them and the control starts lying.

At 864px the rail wants a share of the row rather than shrinking to its labels, so it comes out than today's — the one width where this option costs rather than saves.

Exactly what was asked for: a sticky icon, top right, reachable from anywhere in the board. The ADP block's own material — the chamfered billet, the one part in the app that is a solid rather than a face — at 34px.

It says where you are

The date rides the bottom edge on a nameplate, the trade card's own device, so a travelled board is legible without opening the control. At the top of the board there is no bound, so the key is unlit and the plate isn't drawn.

What it is

The bar is already pinned and already top-right, and this page already fills its one seat with the ADP block. A 30px key beside it is sticky by construction: no floating layer, no z-order, nothing covering a card.

Still one occupant

The seat rule is one occupant per route, not one part: TradesHome renders both from its single <HeaderSlot>. The wordmark's text already hides at this width when the seat is filled, so the room is there.

What it costs

A 390px bar then carries a mark, a page chip, two page controls and the tools key. It fits — just — and there is no room under it for a plate, so a travelled board says so only by the key being lit.

The bar belongs to every tool. Two page-owned controls in it is the point at which the seat becomes a tray; worth deciding on purpose rather than drifting into.

What it is

J1's seat, upgraded from a button into an instrument that says where you are: it carries the date of the card at the top of the viewport. The pattern the ADP trigger already follows — it names its board and opens it.

What it buys

Scrolling stops being blind. "Where am I in time" is a real question on a chronological board a hundred thousand rows deep, and nothing on the page answers it today.

What it costs

The only option with new plumbing: it reads the topmost visible item off the virtualizer and updates on scroll. Cheap, but a wire that does not exist yet.

Update it on isScrolling boundaries, not every frame — a number changing under a flick reads as noise rather than as a position.

If it were mine: A + J1, with J3 as the follow-up

  1. A is the only one that makes the page teach its own most important idea — that the circles are a radius — and it does it with a length rather than a sentence, while keeping every circle one press away. It is not the shortest: measured, it comes out on a phone and at 864px. If height is what matters most, that is B — at the price of putting the page's widest claim behind a press.
  2. J1 is right for the reason it was asked for: the seek is a position, the board is a hundred thousand rows deep, and a position control that scrolls away is a control used once. The nameplate under it is what stops a travelled board from lying about where it is.
  3. J3 is J1 plus the thing that would actually change how the board reads. Same part, same seat, one wire more — so start at J1 and grow into it rather than choosing.
  4. B is what to take instead if the row has to get shorter still. D is the best-looking and I would resist it: three presses to reach a named circle is a real cost paid every session, and this is not a control anyone tunes for fun.

What changes in the code, either way: TRADE_CIRCLES gains a short key label and is reordered narrowest-first — the wire values are untouched, so nothing server-side moves — and circleNote loses its "quiet on the widest circle" branch. SeekField leaves TradeControls for a key rendered by TradesHome, still inside the headerRef box so the virtualizer keeps measuring it. The travel-scrolls-to-the-header effect, tradeSeekBounds, the query key and every count stay exactly as they are.
