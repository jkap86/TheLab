Trades board · the two bays, with a manager at each end

Darkside gave Nabers to jkap

Naming one end answers which way. Naming both answers who to — and it is the question a board of fifty thousand trades is usually being asked: what did my leaguemate get for him, and who from. Three ways to let a manager stand in either bay.

It costs nothing in the query and one thing in the layout. A manager in a bay is not something that side received — it is whose side it is, so the uniform “everything in a bay is what that side got” stops being literally true the moment a second manager lands. Each option below decides how loudly to say that.

What each bay is called, and who decides

Read first

The labels are the whole feature. A bay is a side of the trade; what it is called resolves from whoever is named in either bay, so the control reads as a sentence without ever being written as one. Three states, and the reader crosses all three in one search:

Both ends is free

Each bay binds one of the trade's own roster_ids and asks everything it holds against that id; a named manager adds a primary-key lookup in rosters. Correlated, not decorrelatable — the shape that measured 9ms where writing it FROM rosters inwards measured 205ms, because only this one leaves the board's ORDER BY on its index.

Two managers, one bay

A side has one owner, so two names in one bay can only mean either of them. Read as any of and the bay stays useful — “did either of my two leaguemates land him”. Read as and it matches nothing, forever.

The thing to get right

An empty bay must keep meaning don't care. Every state on this page has to degrade to the board a reader has today when they ignore the second half.

What it buys

It is already built. The bays hold tokens; this lets a manager be one of them. No new part, no new slot, nothing new on a phone.

Several managers in a bay is expressible and useful — did either of these two land him — where a one-per-side slot would have to refuse it.

permission, not a part

What it costs

Two kinds of claim wear one material. A manager token says whose side this is and an asset token says what it got, and nothing but the avatar distinguishes them — so the bay's own label has to carry the difference in words.

The reader has to learn that two managers means or while two players means and. True, useful, and invisible.

What it buys

The distinction is structural, so no label has to carry it. Who a side is and what it got are different slots, which is what they are, and the impossible state — two owners of one side — cannot be typed.

It completes the argument the bays were chosen for: the control is the card, down to the avatar and the order of the lines. A reader who has scrolled the board has already been taught this control.

The got is printed rather than derived, so each bay states its own direction without depending on what the other bay knows.

nothing to explain

What it costs

A line per bay, always — the tallest of the three, and the head is drawn whether or not anyone is named.

One manager per side, so either of these two leaguemates needs a second search or a slot that accepts a small set. That is a real question this shape makes awkward.

a head per bay, drawn empty or not

What it buys

The only one that can describe a three-way, which the board carries and the card already draws. Two bays can say “someone else got Bowers” only by leaving it out.

It is what B becomes on a phone anyway, so shipping it means one layout at every width rather than two.

N sides, one layout

What it costs

It optimises the rare case. Nearly every trade has two sides and nearly every question has two ends, and a stack of full-width rows is a worse two-ended control than two bays — the two halves stop being comparable at a glance.

The swap has nowhere natural to sit once sides are a list, and “side 3” is a number a reader has to keep in their head rather than a place on the screen.

worse at the case that always happens

True in all three

Decisions that don't depend on the shape

Sides are distinct, not exhaustive

Two named sides mean these two were on opposite sides, never this trade had exactly two. A three-way where a third roster took something still matches — the same reading the card takes, and the only one that doesn't silently hide trades.

An empty bay means don't care

One side filled is today's board with a direction on it; none filled is today's board. Nothing about the second half may narrow anything until something is put in it.

The swap is one press, and it is not decoration

“Did Darkside give Nabers to jkap” and the reverse are different questions with the same four tokens, so flipping the bays has to be cheaper than re-picking them.

All/any moves inside a bay

Across bays the relation is structural, so the mode has nothing left to say globally. Within one it still does — any of Nabers, Gibbs went that way — which is where the existing control belongs once this ships.

The counts stay counted without the selection

A result row still says how many trades that name moved in, not how many match the bay you are about to drop it into. Counting per bay re-runs a season-wide aggregate per keystroke, and a menu counted over its own selection collapses to it.

The matched assets get marked on the card

With both ends named, a card can match on four things at once. The board's own highlight is what tells a reader why this trade is in front of them — and on a three-way it is the only thing that can.

If you want a recommendation

B. Once a manager can stand in either bay, the uniform token model is carrying two different claims on one material — whose side this is and what that side got — and the only thing separating them is an avatar. B makes that difference structural, which is what it is, and it finishes the argument the bays were picked for: the control becomes the card, avatar and name over what they took, so the list underneath is the documentation.

What B gives up is either of these two leaguemates, and it is worth being deliberate about that rather than discovering it later: if that question matters, the who-slot takes a small set of names instead of one and B keeps everything else. That is a smaller change than adopting A.

C is right about something neither of the others answers — a third side is real, and it is what every one of these becomes on a phone anyway. It is the wrong default because it is worse at the two-sided case, which is nearly all of them. Ship B, and let a third bay appear when a reader asks for one — that is C when it is needed and B the rest of the time.
