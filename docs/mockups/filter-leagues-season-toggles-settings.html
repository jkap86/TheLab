Shared dialog · manager tabs, trades board, ADP board · three changes

A season at the top, toggles on the face, a settings bay where league size was

Three asks, and they are not three independent edits. The season row needs a population that spans seasons before it can narrow anything. The toggles put back the ~150px the collapsed rows were introduced to save. And "settings" is a much larger vocabulary than "size" — large enough that where the key list comes from matters more than what the bay is called.

What each ask disturbs

Read first
Season

Two of the three callers hand this dialog one season already. /api/user/[username]/leagues?season= and /api/trades/leagues?season= both resolve to a single season server-side; only /api/adp/leagues accepts ?season=all.

So a season row is a live filter on one caller of three today. On the other two it draws a rail with one key on it — which is honest, and not what was asked for. Making it real on the manager tabs is a route change, not a dialog change.

Toggles

The three rows are collapsed because they were toggles. Three captions and thirteen keys is ~290px of a 700px phone spent on what is usually one selection, and it pushed the rule bays — the part of the dialog that can't be asked any other way — below the fold.

Toggles are the better control at every width the dialog is actually read at. The question is only what the phone pays, and whether the bays still open above the fold when it pays it. Measured below: they do, by a margin.

Settings

The whole settings blob is already on the wire. ManagerLeague.settings is Record<string, unknown> and crosses in full; today only type and best_ball are ever read out of it.

So this bay costs no route change, no migration and no payload growth — it is a reader and a key menu. It is the cheapest of the three asks and the one with the most in it.

One thing worth saying before the options. size is already a rule list with exactly one key (teams), built as a list rather than a single comparison precisely so a band — teams ≥ 10 and teams ≤ 12 — is expressible. Renaming it settings and giving it more keys is the shape it was drawn for. Nothing about the AND-only rule, the null-is-not-zero rule or the epsilon comparison changes.

A

Where the season goes

It is asked for at the top, and it belongs there. What is in question is whether it sits in the trough with the other three or above it — and the answer follows from what kind of question it is.

What it buys

One mechanism, four rows, nothing new to build. The season joins FIXED_FILTERS, which is the array activeFilters already walks — so the count on the trigger, the words on the header plate and the clearable chip in the match rail all come for free rather than as three more edits.

What it costs

It says the season is the same kind of question as the other three, and it isn't. Status, type and format are attributes of a league; the season is which population you are looking at — the distinction the ADP drawer is already built around ("the season is the board's population; the window is a cut inside it"). Filed as a peer, a reader reads it as a fourth attribute and never notices that changing it swaps the corpus out.

What it buys

The material says which of the two it is. A lit leading rail and a raised face over the recessed trough is the app's existing grammar for "this is the thing the rest is read against" — the same relationship the header plate has to the list under it.

It also makes the counts underneath honest without a word of explanation. Every number in the trough is already probed against the rest of the draft, so once a season is picked they are counts within that season; the band being visibly upstream is what says so.

What it costs

A second material in a panel that had one, and ~46px that the trough would have absorbed for free. On a phone it wraps to two lines (keys, then the sentence), which is ~72px.

It is still a LeagueFilters field and still walked by activeFilters — the band is a seating decision, not a second mechanism. Nothing downstream can tell the difference.

A3  The degenerate case — one season in hand

Drawn anyway

Season 2026 118 This tool reads one season at a time.

A control with one option, permanently lit. It reports a fact rather than offering a choice — which is the thing every other row in this panel was shortened to stop doing.

Drawn only when there are two

Season — not rendered —

The rule: the band draws when the leagues in hand carry more than one season. Self-scaling, exactly as the scoring bay's key menu is read off the leagues rather than listed — and it turns on by itself the day a caller widens its fetch.

The honest read

Whichever seating wins, the band only does something where the population spans seasons. That is the ADP board today. To make it do something on the manager tabs, /api/user/[username]/leagues has to answer more than one season — either ?season=all or a list — and the leagues stream, the five dependent reads keyed on it and manager_league_order (which is keyed per manager per season) all have an opinion about that.

That is a second piece of work, and worth doing separately. The dialog can ship the band now, live on the ADP board and self-hiding elsewhere, and light up on the other two the moment their routes widen.

What it buys

Thirteen counts at rest, no press, and on a laptop it is shorter than the collapsed rows — 118px against 124 — because a caption and its options share a line instead of stacking a summary above a panel.

It also deletes machinery: the open-row state, the outside-press dismissal, the preventDefault on the dialog's cancel that stops Escape closing everything at once, and the focus return. Four behaviours a floating part owed, owed by nothing.

What it costs

The phone. At 318px of usable width the keys wrap two or three deep, so the trough goes ~150px → ~272px, and with the season band that is ~344px of a 607px scroll box.

The bays still open above the fold — the first bay's head lands at ~360px, comfortably on screen — so the failure that produced the collapsed rows does not come back. What comes back is scrolling to reach the third bay, which is ordinary.

B2 — rails on a laptop, rows on a phone (rejected)

B3 — rails with the counts dropped on a phone (rejected)

Label-only keys fit two lines instead of three, saving ~48px. But the count is the reason to open the dialog rather than guess, and a phone is exactly where a reader is least willing to guess and press. Spend the pixels.

Where the key menu comes from

Off the leagues in hand, exactly as the scoring keys are. What a league configures is a house rule, and a fixed list would offer keys nobody sets while hiding the one someone wants — the argument that already built scoringKeyOptions. A label table (SETTING_KEYS) only ranks and names them; an unranked key still appears, spelled with its underscores knocked out.

That is also what makes the bay safe to ship without a survey of the corpus: it can only ever offer what the leagues on screen actually carry.

What it costs

Sleeper omits what a league doesn't set, so absent has to be read per key and cannot have one rule. disable_trades absent means trades are enabled (a real 0); taxi_slots absent means no taxi squad (a real 0); a whole missing settings blob is unknown and fails every rule, which is the k = 0 trap the slot rules already keep null for.

unverified enum codings — waiver_type's 0/1/2 and pick_trading's flag still want one pass over the stored blobs before they are named on screen. trade_deadline is settled: 99 means no deadline, which is a sentinel rather than a week and is the third value kind below.

The named-value row  ·  what makes a boolean readable

Number field for everything

disable trades = 1 5×
waiver type = 2 88×

Correct, and unreadable. Nothing on screen says whether 1 means disabled or enabled, and the match rail's chip would read disable trades = 1 — a filter a reader cannot check.

A value menu where the key has one

Trades is Disabled ▼ 5×
Waivers is FAAB ▼ 88×

Same FilterRule underneath — {key, op, value}, a number, an epsilon comparison. Only the rendering of the value changes, and the op menu narrows to is / is not because > on an enum is a question with no meaning.

The rule that keeps this from spreading: a key gets a value menu when its numbers are labels (a flag, an enum), and a number field when they are quantities (teams, weeks, slots, budget). A quantity with a menu can only offer the values someone thought of; a label with a number field can only be read by someone who already knows the coding.

The third kind  ·  trade_deadline = 99 is not week 99

99 read as a week

Trade deadline ≤ 12 47×
Trade deadline ≥ 13 71×
of those 71, 47 have no deadline at all

One of these two is right by luck. 99 ≤ 12 is false, so the early-deadline question happens to work; 99 ≥ 13 is true, so "leagues that trade late" quietly answers with every league that never stops trading — a filter returning the wrong rows rather than an error.

99 read as an absence

Trade deadline ≥ 13 24×
Trade deadline is No deadline ▼ 47×
both questions answerable, neither by luck

The reader returns null for 99, so every comparison fails on a league with no deadline — and the sentinel gets a named entry in the same value menu the flags use, matched by identity rather than through compare. That is what the No trade deadline quick-add writes.

So there are three value kinds, not two: a quantity (teams, slots, budget), a label (a flag, an enum), and a quantity carrying a sentinel — a real scale with one value on it that isn't on the scale. Only the third needs both controls at once, and trade_deadline is the only key on the table that is one.

Why this is the same bug twice

sizeValue already returns null for total_rosters === 0, because a zero there is a row stored before the league answered and teams < 10 would sweep every one of them in. slotCount returns null for an unsynced lineup so k = 0 doesn't mean "leagues without a kicker, plus every league we haven't read".

99 is that shape a third time, with one difference worth keeping: those two are genuinely unknown and this one is a known answer wearing a number. So it is null for comparison and reachable by name — the second half is what the other two don't need.

What it costs

A third row shape, and a sentinel field on the key table beside values. The rule underneath is still {key, op, value} — the sentinel rule is trade_deadline eq 99 stored, drawn as is · No deadline — so nothing changes in activeFilters, the trigger count or the match rail's chip, which reads "no trade deadline" rather than "trade deadline = 99".

It also means a key's value kind has to be declared rather than inferred. An unranked key falling through to a plain quantity is still right — it just can't know about a sentinel nobody has told it about, which is the honest default.

If it were mine: A2 + B1 + C1, and the season route as a follow-up

B1 and C1 are unambiguous wins and independent of everything else. The toggle rails are shorter than the collapsed rows on a laptop, put every count on screen, and delete four behaviours a floating panel owed; the settings bay is a rename, a key table and a value renderer over a rule shape that already exists, with the blob already on the wire. Neither needs a route, a migration or a payload change.

A2 is the right seating and the season is the one ask with a dependency behind it. Ship the band drawing off the seasons present in the leagues in hand: live on the ADP board, self-hiding on the other two. Then widen /api/user/[username]/leagues — which is its own piece of work, since manager_league_order is keyed per season and five dependent reads hang off that stream — and the band lights up with no dialog change at all.

  1. Rails first. SegmentRow becomes FilterRail; the trough, the counts and the probe are untouched. Delete openGroup, toggleGroup, closeGroup, the trough ref and the cancel guard. The ADP board's fourth row (draft kind) becomes a countless rail, unchanged in every other way.
  2. Settings bay. LeagueFilters.size → settings; SIZE_KEYS → a ranked label table; a settingKeyOptions(leagues) beside scoringKeyOptions; matchesSizeRule → matchesSettingRule with the per-key absent rule. Presets rewritten.
  3. Named values. SettingKey.values as an optional table; RuleRow renders a menu instead of a number field when it is present, and narrows the op menu to eq/ne.
  4. Sentinels. SettingKey.sentinel beside it — trade_deadline's 99. The reader returns null for it so every comparison fails, and the value menu carries it by name so "no trade deadline" is still askable. Without both halves, trade_deadline ≥ 13 answers with 71 leagues of which 47 have no deadline at all.
  5. Verify the remaining codings against the stored blobs before naming any enum on screen — one query over leagues.settings. Unverified keys ship as plain quantities, which is never wrong, only terse. trade_deadline is confirmed; waiver_type and pick_trading are not.
  6. Season band, options derived from the leagues in hand, hidden below two seasons. Add season to FIXED_FILTERS so the trigger count, the plate summary and the rail chip all follow.
  7. Then the multi-season leagues read, as its own change.
