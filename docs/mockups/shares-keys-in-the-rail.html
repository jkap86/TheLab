The shares keys read as part of the search field

Three parts sit side by side in the rail and all three wear the same milled channel. One of them becomes a text field; the other two throw a full-page sheet over the list. The material is telling the reader they are the same kind of thing, and the material is the first thing read.

What is actually colliding

Read first

The app already has a grammar for this and the rail is the one place it is not being kept: raised means press me, recessed means you are here. A slot cut into the ledge is right for a column heading and right for the search — the search becomes a field, so a channel is what it should look like before it does. The two shares keys are not fields at any point in their life. They open a dialog, which everywhere else in this app is what a raised pill does.

Material

A channel is the app's mark for a readout or a field — headings sit in one, the current-page chip in the app bar is one. A pill is the mark for a press.

Grouping

Three parts of one size at a 7px gap with no divider is a set. Nothing in the row says the first one is a different question from the other two.

Consequence

A reader who thinks all three are fields presses the first and types. The two that would have answered the question in one press go unused.

Why it fits

Adjacency is most of what made them read as one set, and separation is the cheapest thing that undoes it. The trailing end is also where they belong: the count says how many leagues are left and the browses are the two ranked lists of what is in them — all three describe the population, where the field on the left is the reader's own input. It is the argument that moved the ADP block next to Tools rather than next to the page chip.

What it costs

The count stops holding the far edge — it moves inboard of two keys, so the rail's own answer is no longer the last thing on the line. And below sm the storey wraps, which turns "one end / the other end" into "first line / second line": still legible, but the spatial argument is gone at the width it was needed most.

Pick A, and take B with it

They are not rivals — A fixes what the parts are and B fixes what they belong with, and the two together cost about eight lines and no new CSS. Material carries it at every width, which is what matters, since the width where the confusion is worst is the width where B's spatial argument wraps away. C and D are the interesting ones to argue about, but each spends something real: C spends a press on the app's most common path into the sheet, D spends a new part on a vocabulary that was deliberately narrowed.

  1. Swap the two keys' class from lab-ledge-slot … rounded-[3px] px-2 py-[3px] to lab-chip lab-chip-sm rounded-full px-2 py-[3px] — the ADP drawer's small key, already in the stylesheet.
  2. Move them after the count in the storey, with the count keeping its ml-auto and a hairline divider between.
  3. Leave the labels alone. They already contract to Players / Leaguemates once a token appears, which is the width case that matters.
  4. Check the pair on the real face. A chip is darker than this storey, unlike a chip on the page ground — if the lit edge does not carry it, lighten the chip's top stop rather than reaching for a border.

True whichever is chosen

The search stays a channel

It is the one part in the storey that becomes a text field, and a recessed slot is the honest thing for it to look like before it does.

Both keys still open a modal

aria-haspopup="dialog" stays on them, and the search trigger keeps aria-expanded — that difference is already correct in the markup and is exactly the one the material was failing to draw.

Icons are the last resort, not the fix

The three glyphs already differ and did not prevent this. An icon distinguishes two things the reader is already looking at; material tells them what kind of thing it is first.

The storey wraps, never compresses

Every part in it is shrink-0, so a phone puts the keys on a second line rather than truncating them. Any option has to survive that break.
