Leagues · the open card

Type, spacing, and the readout without the gap

Three asks, and one of them turns out to be free. The panel is set in Arial — while the repository self-hosts Geist and Geist Mono, preloads both on every page, and wires neither to anything. Two lines of CSS recover them. Below that: four ways to spend the roster column's vertical budget, three ways to hold both stat columns on a phone — which is what rules out the obvious one-line row at that width — and three shapes for the readout strip once the lineup gap comes off it.

Every specimen on this page is rendered in the actual faces from src/app/fonts/, on the actual materials from globals.css. Nothing here is a drawing.

0 What the panel is set in today

Not a design finding so much as a wiring one. src/app/fonts/index.ts loads three faces and exposes them as --font-geist-sans, --font-geist-mono and --font-orbitron. The @theme block in globals.css maps exactly one of the three. A grep for the other two across src/ returns nothing.

Today

Body text is Arial by an explicit body rule. Every font-mono in the app falls through to Tailwind's default stack, so the readouts are Menlo on a Mac, Consolas on Windows and DejaVu Sans Mono on Android — a different instrument per reader.

1 The type — three depths

Each of these is cumulative: A2 includes A1, A3 includes A2. The strip and four roster rows are shown under each so the change is read on the thing it changes rather than on a specimen sheet.

A1 · Turn on what already ships two lines of css

The face changes and nothing else does — no size, no weight, no spacing. This is the whole of §0 applied. It is the option with no design argument in it at all, which is exactly why it should land on its own before anything else moves.

A2 · A1 plus the panel's own type scale recommended

The readout labels are 7px at 0.1em tracking and 30% opacity — below where uppercase stays crisp on any face, which is why PROJECTED reads as grey fuzz in the screenshot rather than as a word. A2 lifts the four smallest strings in the panel to sizes their own material can carry, and gives the digits one step of size back.

Nothing gets taller: the readout cell grows 2px of type and loses 1px of padding either side. The section titles and column headings are unchanged in position, only in weight and opacity.

Orbitron on the readout units and the section titles — never on a name and never on a number. This is the app's existing rule for a named row (display face, one size step down, because Orbitron is wider), applied to the panel's own furniture.

2 The players column — four ways to spend the height

A roster row is two lines today, and the second line carries three things: a position badge, an NFL team, and the two numbers. On a starter's row the badge repeats the slot tab directly above it — the screenshot shows QB stacked on QB, RB on RB — so a whole line is being spent on a duplicate, and the numbers end up a line below the name they belong to with the name line's right half empty. That is ~49px per player, ~1,960px for a 40-man roster.

Four options, from the smallest diff to the boldest. Each is rendered at the two widths that matter: the desktop half (~340px) and the phone half (~168px), which is the width the screenshot was taken at and the one where the crowding actually shows. Every panel in this section is set in Geist, including the ones labelled "today" — §1 is where the type is argued, and holding it constant here is what makes these comparisons about height alone. Both stat columns are on every row at every width, here and in §3; the phone renders below are previews of the problem, and §3 is where it is solved.

B1 · Tighten the rhythm smallest diff

Same architecture, less air and one less line of chrome: row padding 6px → 4px, the 2px inter-line gap goes, and the hairline rule between every pair of rows is replaced by a 1.7% band on alternate rows. A rule per row is 40 horizontal lines down a roster; banding says the same thing without drawing anything.

49px → 41px per row. A 40-man roster loses ~320px.

The duplicate is the thing to remove, not the line. A starter's mark carries the slot and takes the position's tone, so the badge's colour coding survives without the badge — FLEX in emerald says "a running back is in the flex" in one part where the panel used two. B4 · One line, one mark, spent on air the calm one

B3's architecture, but the height it frees is given back as breathing room rather than banked as density: 8px rows, no rule of any kind between rows, 16px between sections, and the two number columns sitting in one recessed lane down the right — the trades card's own device, which is what makes a run of digits read as an instrument rather than as loose text.

49px → 36px per row. Less dense than B3, still a quarter shorter than today, and the quietest of the four.

Why the lane

With the rules gone there is nothing left saying where a row ends — so the alignment has to come from somewhere. A recessed lane behind the numbers does it without drawing 40 horizontal lines: the eye tracks the edge of the cut instead of a border. It is also the one place in this panel where the trades card's .lab-groove grammar transfers exactly — a column that is read rather than acted on sits in a cut.

  • The quietest of the four. Fewest drawn lines per screen of any option here, today's included.
  • The lane gives the numbers a permanent home, so a metric with a short value (0.00) does not read as a hole.
  • Half the density win of B3 — a 40-man roster is ~1,440px rather than ~1,200px.
  • One more material inside the panel, on a surface that already nests plate → plate-sm.

M1 · Two lines, but the second one earns its keep recommended

The phone's problem was never that the row has two lines — it is that the second line is spent on a badge repeating the tab above it. Take the badge out (the mark carries the slot and the position's colour, B3's move) and line two holds only what belongs to it. Three things follow, and all three are gains:

  • The numbers get bigger, not smaller. 0.65rem → 0.7rem, which is 42.7px against the 44px track already there — the size the code comment measured and could not previously afford.
  • The phone gets the NFL team back. It is hidden … @lg:inline today because the badge was using that space. It fits easily now: team ~22px plus 88px of numbers plus gaps is 126px of 154.
  • The row gets shorter anyway. A 17px badge with its padding leaves and a 14px line of text arrives: ~49px → ~42px, without touching the rhythm.

What moved

The mark is still on the row's leading corner, out of flow, so it costs the name an indent and nothing else — exactly as today. The badge is gone because the mark now says the same thing in colour. Line two is the team on the left and the two numbers on the right, which is a line with a left edge and a right edge rather than three things fighting over the middle.

The headings can go back to their full words too — Start / Bench rather than St / Bn — because the track was always wide enough and only the rail's own type size was in question.

  • Nothing documented is reversed. Both value columns stay at every width, the name keeps its own line at every width, and the panel keeps one row architecture from 166px to 400px.
  • It is the same edit as B3 — remove the badge, tone the mark — so the desktop and the phone are one change rather than two.
  • Still two lines, so the density win is ~14% rather than B3's ~39%. On a phone that is the honest ceiling.

The trade

The panel's own note says the halves stay side by side at every width because reading one against the other is the point. The counter-argument is that at 166px each you are not reading one against the other — you are reading two cramped lists, and full player names have not been on screen at all.

What it costs is that the standings need a ceiling of their own, or a twelve-team table pushes the roster off the bottom of the card. A capped, scrolling standings block above the roster is the straightforward version; a collapsed one-row team picker is the other, and that is a bigger change than this document is scoped for.

  • The only arrangement where a phone gets a one-line row, full player names, and both stat columns at once.
  • The desktop is untouched — this is a below-@sm arrangement only.
  • Reverses "even 50/50 split at every width", which is written down and argued for in league-detail-panel.tsx.
  • Needs a height policy for the standings block that does not exist yet. That is the part that makes this a bigger job than M1 or M2.

4 The readout strip without the lineup gap

Removing the third cell is one deletion. What it leaves behind is a decision: a dial and two wells is a different-shaped object from a dial and three, and the strip currently wraps to a second line on a phone precisely because of the third.

Nothing else has to change. points_left is still read by the lineup checker's gap column and by the standings' week-projection hover, so the contract, the solver and the payload all stay exactly as they are — this is the panel deciding not to state it, not the app forgetting how to compute it.

Three loose wells minus one reads as a missing part. Two numbers that are always read together read better as one instrument — which is exactly what .lab-bezel / .lab-gauge already are on the trade card: a trough milled into the face with a window sunk into its floor per value, each carrying the unit underneath.
