The bay rail, and what the float covers

B's rail of keys and C's floating body, built together — and the pair beats both halves, because B's rail is one line where C's rows are three. The pinned block goes from today's 314px to roughly 100, and the board from 9 rows to 17.

Which changes what there is left to argue about. Every variant below has the same small constant block, so the number that decides between them is how much board the open bay hides. Each panel measures that per bay, live — open a bay and watch the coverage table fill in.

Measuring it retired the objection I raised against a floating bay last time. The tallest bay — the window — is 197px, so it hides five rows and twelve are still readable underneath it. Today's block shows nine, with nothing open at all. A float over this rail leaves more board than the current design ever offers, which means "it covers the board" was an argument about the wrong quantity: what matters is what is left, and there is more of it here in every state.

Raised means press me; recessed means you are here. So a rail key is a raised pill, and the key you opened sinks into the rail while its bay stands above it. 04H4 — Rail at the foot

The one arrangement that answers the coverage problem outright. The rail sits above the footer, so bays rise — and what they cover is the bottom of the board. 05Verdict

H3 — and H4 if the coverage bothers you

The hybrid is right, and it is a bigger win than either half: the block drops from 314px to ~100 and the board from 9 rows to 17, with nothing ever reflowing. That is C's constant height, B's single line, and none of B's dead space.

And the objection I raised against the float last time does not survive being measured. The window bay hides five rows and leaves twelve; the current design shows nine with nothing open. There is no state of this drawer where a reader sees less board than they see today, which is what makes the float affordable rather than merely tolerable.

Between the four, H2's keyed floats are strictly better than H1's full-width one — same window bay, much smaller Leagues and Curve, and a tongue that says which key a panel belongs to. That part is not really a choice.

H3 is H2 with the one control that must not float taken out of the float. The curve is a slider, so it fits the rail; a reader drags it with the whole board visible and the value columns bending underneath, which is the drawer's entire premise. What is left floating is Leagues and Window — both genuinely settings, both read-then-dismiss, both fine behind a press.

H4 lost most of its case to the measurement. It was built to answer "the bay covers the rows you are watching", and the answer turned out to be that five rows are covered and twelve remain — more than today shows with nothing open. H4 protects rows 1–8 absolutely, but it hides seven rows to do it (a bay hanging from the head spends some of its height on the column headings; one rising from the foot spends all of it on rows), and it costs a second strip of chrome and the distance between the window control and the draft count that control moves. Take it if seeing H2 in use says the top five rows matter more than that; the measurement says they don't.

Whichever way, five things to hold to:

  1. Sunk means open, cyan means narrowed. Two signals, two meanings — a key that went cyan on open would spend the accent on a state the tongue and the sink already say.
  2. A key states its value, always. Leagues carries the rule count and the draft kind; Window carries its label and a density sparkline; Curve carries what it does to a board. A rail of three bare nouns is the trades ledge mistake in a smaller box.
  3. The board stays live under an open bay — it scrolls, the rows re-price as the bay is used, and a press on it closes the bay. A float that freezes the board is a modal wearing a panel's clothes.
  4. Escape closes the innermost thing. The bay first, the drawer second — the league-filters dialog already owes this to its segment rows, so the drawer's own cancel has to be preventDefaulted while a bay is up.
  5. The window bay opens on a cold drawer. It is the first thing the drawer is opened for, and the one bay whose collapse this whole exercise is in tension with. Once a window is set, it remembers being shut.
