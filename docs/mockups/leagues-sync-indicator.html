Sync state · manager header

Four places for the flask

The sync line is a row in the plate's flow, so the card grows when a refresh starts and shrinks when it ends — and the whole list under it moves both times. Its spinner is also a generic ring, on a page that has its own loading mark.

All four options below are absolutely positioned, so the plate's height never changes, and all four use FlaskLoader. What differs is where the indicator sits and how much of the message it can carry.

What has to stay true

constraints
four states, two kinds

Refreshing is transient and self-resolving. Partial, locked and failed are standing caveats about the rows already on screen — they do not go away on their own.

An indicator that treats them as one thing either makes a caveat look like a spinner or makes a spinner look permanent.

the counts must be spoken

97 of 100 is not decoration. A partly-failed sync leaves the list a mix of just-synced rows and rows as old as the last pass, and nothing else on the page can tell it from a complete one.

So no option may put that number behind a hover — which rules out the smallest, tidiest badge.

the plate has no spare surface

Measured on the shipped card: the record bar's bottom sits 10px above the face's bottom edge and the dial's 8px. The two top corners are tabs and the leading edge is the accent rail.

So "absolute" and "covers nothing" cannot both be true for free — the real question every option below answers is what does it cover, or what does it reserve. A first draft of A was drawn straddling the bottom edge and landed squarely on the record bar.

How big a flask has to be

measured, not guessed

FlaskLoader draws in a 120-unit box with bubbles of radius 1.8–3.2 that rise 30 units. Rendered at width W, a bubble is W/120 × r across — so the animation the flask exists for has a floor, and it is higher than a badge.

18px sub-pixel bubbles
26px ~1px, a smudge
34px just visible
44px reads as bubbling
56px unmistakable

Below ~34px the flask is a mark, not a loader. That is not fatal — a static flask still says "The Lab is working", and the reduced-motion block in globals.css deliberately ships exactly that. But it means "use the flask" and "use the flask animating" are different asks, and only option C can honour the second one.

The pulse variant is drawn throughout: its glowing rim is the one variant whose motion survives at small sizes, because the glow is a whole-outline opacity breath rather than a 1px dot travelling.

What it buys

It reuses a device the app already owns — the league card's name plate rides its top edge for exactly this reason — so the indicator reads as part of the plate rather than as something dropped on it.

It covers nothing — once it is given a lane. The bottom edge is the only edge with no readout on it, but it is not free: the record bar ends 10px above it. 12px of permanent bottom padding clears it, and a constant 12px is not the layout shift this change is about. The card is the same height whether a sync is running or not, which is the rule the record bar's own empty rail already keeps.

covers nothing · fits every message

What it costs

12px of card height, permanently, on a card that is above a list — paid whether or not anything is ever syncing. That is the trade, and it is the whole of it.

It must be a sibling of the slab, never a child. clip-path clips a whole subtree, so a plate rendered inside the face would be severed at the exact edge it exists to straddle. The relative wrapper in manager-header.tsx is already there for this — its comment says so.

The flask is 22px, so it is a mark rather than a loader. And an amber nameplate is a new material: the caution tone has only ever been a bordered text pill here, never a plate.

What it buys

It is the most literally correct thing in the app's own grammar: a recessed cell whose housing lights because the value in it is running is exactly what .lab-readout-live was written for on the countdown.

Seated inside the face, the chamfer cuts it the same way it cuts the two top tabs — so all four corners of the plate are wells, and the card stays one part.

What it costs

It covers the dial — look at the mock above, not at this sentence. The dial's bottom is 8px off the face's bottom edge, so a corner cell tall enough to hold a flask and a count takes the WIN PCT caption and the bottom of the ring with it. This was written as "crowds on a narrow viewport" before it was rendered; it crowds at every width.

The longer messages ("Syncing elsewhere — this list may be incomplete") do not fit a corner at all, so they truncate or push further across the card — which covers more of the dial, not less.

It also needs an amber sibling of -live, which globals.css does not have.

What it buys

The flask is legible. At 56px the bubbles rise and pop and the rim breathes — this is the only option where "use the loading flask" buys the animation rather than the silhouette.

It follows a decision the plate already made. That slot is where the countdown and the dial trade places, on the argument that they are never both worth drawing; a refresh in flight is a third reading of the same "what is live right now".

the only legible flask

What it costs

It hides the win percentage for as long as a refresh runs, which on a cold hundred-league account is a while. The dial is the card's one verdict, and covering it to report a process is arguably backwards.

It also does not solve the caveats — those still need somewhere to go, so this option is really "C plus A", and two indicators is one more than the card had.

What it buys

It is the only option with no geometry to negotiate: no chamfer, no corner tab, no wall arithmetic. It can also say anything at any length, since nothing constrains its width but the page.

What it costs

It belongs to no part. Everything else on this page is seated in something — that is the whole of the material grammar — and a capsule floating on its own reads as a notification from the browser rather than as a reading from the instrument.

It also covers the stat corner tab at this position, and would cover a league card's own plate anywhere lower. Being out of flow does not stop it being in the way; it just moves the cost somewhere less predictable.

covers the stat tab

Where this lands

the recommendation

A — because the thing it pays is a constant

Every option here costs something; A's cost is the one that never surprises anybody. The plate has no spare surface, so an indicator either covers a reading (B covers its own corner and truncates, C covers the dial, D covers whatever it lands on) or reserves a lane. A reserves 12px, permanently, and in exchange covers nothing, fits the longest message, and holds the card at one height forever — which is the rule this card already keeps for the record bar out of season.

The flask at that size is a mark, not a loader, and that is the one thing worth deciding deliberately rather than discovering. If the animation is the point, the honest answer is C — but C hides the dial and still needs A underneath it for the caveats, so it buys the bubbling at the price of two indicators and the card's only verdict.

B is the most orthodox and the least practical. Its grammar is exactly right — a housing that lights because what is in it is running — and its corner cannot hold "Syncing elsewhere — this list may be incomplete" without truncating, which is the one message that has to be read to mean anything.

worth doing either way

Whichever wins, the refreshing state and the three caveats want different treatments: one is a process with a running count, the others are standing facts about the rows on screen. Today they share a row and a colour and only differ by wording.

the accessibility half

role="status" moves with the indicator, and the running tally stays aria-hidden — a polite region re-reading "Refreshing 41/121" every few hundred milliseconds talks over the page it is describing. That rule survives all four positions.
